What a breakfast egg teaches about bottlenecks

Loops You Can Trust

Where to begin

Eggs, toast and coffee have to arrive together. A faster toaster cannot shorten the three minutes an egg takes. Borrowing Andy Grove’s breakfast example, Tan asks which stage sets the pace when agents work on software.

A closer look

Her Cursor performance work gives one answer. The agent needs to launch a development build, reproduce an interaction, record a profile and repeat the measurement after the change. Sharing the engineer’s instruments makes the investigation repeatable.

What visual parity missed

In a migration to StyleX, captured states looked equivalent. Internal use still revealed stacking problems and consumers of global styles. The next workflow took one leaf component at a time and checked dependencies before removing classes. A failure could then be traced to a much smaller set of decisions.

Editor’s notebook

If the follow-up run cannot reproduce the original conditions, record an inconclusive result. A workflow that can stop there is easier to trust with its next task.
