The delight of making text move

Coding is dead, long live Coding

Where to begin

Tan opens with the HTML marquee tag she found in high school. Making words move offered immediate evidence that she could change something on a screen. That memory explains both the pleasure she took in refining a line of code and the loss she feels as the work changes.

A closer look

She then turns to ideas that used to exceed her reach. Bringing one into the world still requires a clear picture of the result and enough context to communicate it. The change of case in the title invites a reading: which parts of making software would we most like to keep?

Why the shoemaker matters

The shoemaker analogy allows for a craft to survive even as it becomes rare in daily life. Tan then traces a debt to earlier programmers through the languages, frameworks and models available today. That turn gives the hopeful ending some weight. A newcomer’s delight and an experienced programmer’s grief can coexist.

Editor’s notebook

Read this alongside her verification guide. Pick something you genuinely want to make, then describe how you would recognize a good version. The two essays illuminate different halves of that exercise.
