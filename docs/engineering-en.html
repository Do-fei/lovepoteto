From mining ore to laying track

Coding is Dead, Long Live Engineering

Where to begin

Tan’s January argument starts with a condition: engineers must be able to build agents capable of doing the work. A model working inside one repository may have access to years of design decisions and internal search. Move it into a different environment and the same assignment can go very differently.

A closer look

One colleague, in Tan’s account, taught Claude enough about a difficult project to solve a bug that had resisted investigation for more than a year. The interesting question is what the colleague knew and how that knowledge became usable by a tool. The story gives us a concrete case, with no controlled comparison of model performance.

Why the Factorio player stays busy

In Factorio, automating a mine gives the player a railway to lay and a production line to redesign. A poorly arranged factory can still demand constant intervention. The analogy suggests a consequence worth watching: a small decision that once slowed one worker can now hold up an entire production line.

Editor’s notebook

Pair this with the similarly titled September essay. January asks who designs the production system. September asks what someone who loved making things by hand might miss.
