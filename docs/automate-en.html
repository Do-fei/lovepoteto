Start with a job worth handing over

Automate Anything with Grok Bot

Where to begin

Tan takes apart an apparently simple assignment: handling a booking. Filling in a form, sending an email, saving the right time and getting a confirmation to the customer are four distinct states. Once the customer’s outcome is explicit, the bot can help decide which steps the business actually needs.

A closer look

Tan suggests talking the bot through one ordinary working day, including where information lives and how a person checks the result. Hearing the assignment back exposes missing context. In her repair-shop example, service timestamps give a waiting customer’s complaint something concrete to investigate.

What counts as a completed booking?

The booking example includes conflicting times in email and the reservation system, services that need a buffer, and requests that must resume when details arrive. A human can teach those judgments with examples. A schedule or email trigger becomes useful once the bot knows which record to trust and when to ask for help.

Editor’s notebook

Before choosing a tool, name the person who benefits and the change they will notice. Those two sentences can become a first acceptance check.
